Lesson 01

Prime & Composite Numbers

Learn how factors help us classify whole numbers.

🌟 Prime numbers

A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.

Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11

🍑 Composite numbers

A composite number is a whole number greater than 1 with more than two positive factors.

Examples: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10

Remember!

1 is neither prime nor composite. The number 2 is the only even prime number.

Lesson 02

Algebra

Algebra uses letters or symbols to represent unknown numbers.

What is a variable?

In the equation x + 3 = 8, the letter x represents a number we do not know yet.

Example 1: Addition

x + 4 = 10

Subtract 4 from both sides.

x = 6

Example 2: Multiplication

3x = 15

Divide both sides by 3.

x = 5

💡 Helpful hint

Whatever operation you use to solve one side of an equation, use the same operation on the other side to keep it balanced.

Lesson 03

Fractions

Fractions represent parts of a whole.

🍰 Numerator

The top number tells you how many parts are being considered.

3
4

🥧 Denominator

The bottom number tells you how many equal parts make up the whole.

For 3/4, the whole is divided into four equal parts, and three are considered.

Equivalent fractions

Equivalent fractions have the same value.

1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6

Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number.

Lesson 04

Decimals

Decimals represent parts of a whole using place value.

Place value example

4.75

4 = ones, 7 = tenths, 5 = hundredths.

The first digit after the decimal point is the tenths place.

Comparing decimals

0.8 is greater than 0.65 because 0.80 is greater than 0.65.

Adding decimals

Line up the decimal points.

1.25 + 0.50 = 1.75

Lesson 05

Integers

Integers include negative whole numbers, zero, and positive whole numbers.

The number line

-3-2-10123
⬇️

Numbers farther to the right are greater. Numbers farther to the left are smaller.

Adding integers

3 + (-5) = -2

Start at 3 and move 5 spaces left.

Subtracting integers

2 - 5 = -3

Start at 2 and move 5 spaces left.

💡 Remember

Zero is neither positive nor negative.
